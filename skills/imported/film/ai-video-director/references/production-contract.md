# Shared storyboard and prompt contract

Use this contract for the deliverable actually requested. User constraints and approved choices take precedence over optional example structures. Do not present internal ledgers or generic checklists as the finished creative work.

## Storyboard

Represent each real shot as a distinct uninterrupted viewpoint with its narrative purpose, starting and ending state, framing, camera position and movement, visible action, relevant dialogue and sound, cut or handoff, and continuity when needed. A five-column table of time, shot size and position, camera, image and action, and dialogue and sound is a useful default, not a required format. Select only detail needed by the task. Time windows should fit physical actions, performance, speech and the full requested duration; do not divide mechanically or impose a fixed shot count.

## Prompt

Compile the selected shot design rather than making new directing decisions while formatting it. Describe subject and reference roles, scene, physical action, camera, framing, focus, light, timing, sound when supported, and constraints relevant to the selected model and brief. Restate continuity details in each separately generated shot as needed. If the user asks for one continuous prompt, provide one. If the user requests separate shots, give each a usable independent prompt. Platform limits may require a different execution plan; verify the limit and disclose material consequences.

A six-part structure covering generation task, subjects, scene, emotion, style and timed action can help when useful, but is not compulsory. Do not demand ten visible fields, fixed camera metadata in every heading, or extra artifacts the user did not ask for. Preserve dialogue verbatim, subject identity, user locks and selected cinematography. Do not insert unexplained IDs or private paths into user-facing prompts.

## Continuity and physical action

Establish the relevant world geography, blocking, action axis, object state and light positions once. Derive screen position and illumination separately for each camera. Check cut direction as well as geometry: a mathematically correct reversal does not automatically explain a side change to a viewer. Identify who is on screen, who is not, real entrance routes, prop ownership, and the visible transition between states. Ensure contact, force, action, result and relevant environmental reactions make sense. Do not invent camera or platform capabilities.

## Verify

Reconstruct the visible action from the finished storyboard or prompt and compare with the source and selected shot decisions. Check material timing, shot order, human readability, user locks, subject and scene identity, light, space, physical causality and sound cues. Mark missing source or uncertain geometry rather than inventing evidence. For actual generated media, these textual checks are not proof that the model followed the plan or that the final video was watched.
