# Phase 1 Research - Camera Systems and Capture Formats

Status: RESEARCH EVIDENCE

Primary classification: `CONFIRMED` for manufacturer-documented facts, `INFERRED` only where explicitly stated.

This research file collects first-party camera-system evidence that can later be translated into observable image-generation behavior. It deliberately avoids the common mistake of treating a camera-body name as a guaranteed aesthetic preset independent of lens, exposure, lighting, transform, filtration and grade.

## Governing Principle

A real digital cinema camera is primarily a capture system. Its final look depends on a chain including:

- sensor/film format;
- exposure;
- lens and filtration;
- white balance;
- codec/raw/log pipeline;
- color science/display transform;
- lighting and production design;
- grade.

Therefore later runtime rules should use camera names as **references to documented capture tendencies and pipelines**, not magical style tokens.

## ARRI ALEXA 35

Sources: `CAM-ARRI-001`, `CAM-ARRI-002`, `CAM-ARRI-003`.

First-party documented facts relevant to visual translation:

- ARRI documents approximately 17 stops of measured exposure latitude for ALEXA 35;
- high sensitivity capability reaches EI 6400;
- ALEXA 35 introduced/uses REVEAL Color Science with ARRI Wide Gamut 4 and LogC4;
- ARRI emphasizes improved highlight handling, color reproduction and natural skin-tone reproduction;
- ARRI Textures provide in-camera control over aspects of image texture, including how detail/noise/contrast character is rendered at different scales.

### Safe generative translation

When a user explicitly locks `ARRI ALEXA 35`, later rules may favor language such as:

- controlled highlight rolloff;
- retained highlight color rather than brittle clipping;
- open but information-rich shadows when exposure supports them;
- natural skin-tone separation;
- restrained digital sharpness rather than artificial crispness;
- neutral/flexible log-originated color that can support a deliberate grade.

Do **not** say that an image generated by an AI model literally has 17 stops of dynamic range or was captured in LogC4.

## Sony VENICE 2

Source: `CAM-SONY-001`.

First-party documented facts relevant to visual translation:

- VENICE 2 is offered with an 8.6K full-frame sensor and a 6K sensor option;
- the 8.6K sensor exposes dual-base ISO around 800/3200;
- Sony documents approximately 16 stops of latitude for the 8.6K system;
- the 6K sensor option uses dual-base ISO around 500/2500 and Sony documents 15+ stops;
- Sony supports 16-bit X-OCN capture and wide-gamut color workflows.

### Safe generative translation

An expert `VENICE 2` lock can justify an intent toward:

- clean high-end full-frame digital-cinema capture;
- strong low-light flexibility when story conditions require it;
- broad highlight/shadow retention;
- natural color with a gradeable rather than baked-in feel;
- controlled, polished sensor response.

Avoid asserting a unique fixed `Sony look` where the source does not justify one.

## RED V-RAPTOR / V-RAPTOR [X]

Sources: `CAM-RED-001`, `CAM-RED-002`.

First-party facts:

- V-RAPTOR 8K VV uses a large-format/Vista Vision class sensor around 40.96 x 21.60 mm;
- RED publishes 17+ stops as its dynamic-range specification/claim for relevant V-RAPTOR systems;
- V-RAPTOR [X] adds global-shutter capture.

### Safe generative translation

Useful later concepts:

- large-format field-of-view/depth relationships when paired with focal length and camera distance;
- high-resolution contemporary digital capture;
- global-shutter reference may matter for motion-skew language in temporal contexts, but this V1 still skill should not add a visible `global-shutter look` unless motion geometry makes it relevant.

Manufacturer dynamic-range numbers should be represented as documented claims, not cross-brand apples-to-apples ranking.

## Canon EOS C500 Mark II

Source: `CAM-CANON-001`.

First-party whitepaper facts relevant to the skill:

- Canon Log 2 and Canon Log 3 are documented capture/gamma options;
- Canon describes approximately 15 stops with Canon Log 2 and approximately 14 stops with Canon Log 3 under its test/measurement framework;
- Cinema Gamut provides a wide color space for cinema/post workflows.

### Safe generative translation

If the user locks this camera:

- treat it as a modern cinema capture reference with broad highlight/shadow retention;
- keep skin/color natural unless the user requests a grade;
- avoid baking a generic `Canon warm skin` stereotype into every shot without stronger evidence.

## Blackmagic URSA Mini Pro

Sources: `CAM-BMD-001`, `CAM-BMD-002`.

First-party facts:

- URSA Mini Pro variants include different sensors/resolutions and should not be collapsed into one exact spec;
- current/legacy product documentation describes roughly 14 to 15 stops depending on model;
- Blackmagic Generation 5 Color Science is part of the RAW/color pipeline;
- Blackmagic RAW preserves sensor data in a post-friendly workflow.

### Safe generative translation

Use this as a cinema-camera reference, not a fixed LUT/look. If exact model is unspecified, do not invent sensor resolution or dynamic-range number.

## IMAX / 65-70mm

Magnific exposes `IMAX 70mm` as a cinematic control, but Phase 1 did not find a sufficiently authoritative first-party IMAX source that supports a detailed generative `look profile` suitable for runtime rules.

What can safely be carried forward at this stage:

- 65/70mm motion-picture capture represents a very large image area relative to 35mm cinema;
- format size changes field-of-view/depth relationships for equivalent framing;
- user intent often associates the format with large-scale, high-resolution theatrical imagery.

What must **not** be invented yet:

- a fixed IMAX color palette;
- guaranteed shallow depth of field;
- a particular contrast curve;
- a specific grain level without selecting an actual stock and capture process;
- claims about proprietary IMAX camera characteristics unsupported by a source.

This remains a research gap for later refinement.

## Film, Digital and Legacy Capture Are Different Axes

Magnific's camera menu mixes camera bodies with 35mm film, 8mm film, VHS, Pixelvision and medium format.

Our future schema should not repeat that conceptual conflation.

Recommended later separation:

```text
capture_medium: digital | negative_film | reversal_film | analog_video | low-fi-digital
capture_format: S35 | LF/full-frame | VistaVision-class | 65mm | medium-format | 16mm | 8mm | etc.
camera_reference: optional named system
capture_character: observable response requested by user/story
```

This makes expert locks more precise and beginner AUTO decisions more physically coherent.

## Format, Lens and Camera Distance

A camera format does not independently determine perspective.

Later knowledge must preserve this fundamental distinction:

- perspective is primarily determined by camera position relative to the scene;
- focal length and capture format determine field of view for that camera position;
- equivalent framing across formats often leads operators to choose different focal lengths and/or camera positions, which is why `format look` discussions can become confused.

This topic is implemented formally in Phase 3, not here.

## Dynamic Range Translation Rule

Do not convert manufacturer stop counts directly into prompt phrases such as `17 stops of dynamic range` and expect literal simulation.

Translate the relevant intent into visible consequences when justified:

- highlight color retained near bright practicals/windows;
- gradual highlight transition instead of abrupt white clipping;
- shadow detail retained without lifted gray blacks when exposure permits;
- no artificial HDR/local-tone-mapping halos;
- exposure feels like one coherent capture rather than separately optimized foreground/background layers.

## Color Science Translation Rule

Log curves and wide-gamut color spaces are acquisition/post-production encodings, not final looks.

Therefore:

- `LogC4`, `S-Log3`, `Canon Log`, etc. should not automatically make the generated image flat/log-looking unless the user explicitly asks for an ungraded/log frame;
- named-camera AUTO should target a plausible **finished cinematic image** informed by the camera's documented response, then apply the story-appropriate grade;
- if the user requests `ungraded log`, that becomes a separate output intent.

## Manufacturer Marketing Caution

Terms such as `filmic`, `natural`, `organic`, `cinematic`, and `beautiful skin` often appear in manufacturer materials.

Use them only after translating them into observable, testable image traits. Do not copy marketing language into prompts as if it were physics.

## Phase 1 Camera Knowledge Strength

Strong:

- ARRI ALEXA 35 technical/color-science evidence;
- Sony VENICE 2 sensor/latitude/base-ISO evidence;
- RED V-RAPTOR format/spec evidence;
- Canon C500 Mark II log/gamut evidence;
- Blackmagic URSA Mini Pro/BRAW color-pipeline evidence.

Moderate:

- mapping camera-system names to distinctive finished aesthetics after normalization, because lens/light/grade dominate many visible differences.

Weak / unresolved:

- a rigorous first-party IMAX/65mm look profile for generative translation;
- direct, normalized visual comparisons across all camera systems;
- any basis for pretending camera-name prompting alone reproduces measured sensor behavior.

## Requirements Carried Forward

Phase 3 should:

- separate capture medium, format, camera reference and camera character;
- store documented camera facts separately from generative translations;
- never rank cameras by marketing dynamic-range numbers;
- translate capture-system intent into observable highlight/shadow/color/detail behavior;
- keep lens, exposure and grade as independent systems;
- preserve exact user camera locks while allowing AUTO to infer a generic capture character when no hardware is specified.
